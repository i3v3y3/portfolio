## The problem

Most automated machines in Kenya still run on cash. That quietly costs operators
three things at once: revenue leaks to theft and manual reconciliation, nobody
can see how a machine is performing until they physically visit it, and without
a transaction record an operator cannot reach formal credit.

## What I built

Firmware in C++ on FreeRTOS and ESP-IDF that meters out product by volume across
four preset tiers, applies the right price to each, and streams every
transaction back over LTE. The board is a 4-layer design in Altium carrying the
cellular modem, BLE and the sensing interfaces.

## Why 4 layers

Two would have been cheaper per unit. The board carries a cellular modem
alongside low-level sensing, so it needs a continuous ground plane to keep RF
noise off the measurement lines, and a separate power plane to hold rails steady
through transmit bursts. Squeezing that onto two layers means routing around
your own return paths, and the noise shows up as bad readings in the field
rather than on the bench.

## Result

One controller now serves six kinds of machine — water and milk ATMs, coffee,
vending, laundry, pool tables — rather than a bespoke board per vertical. The
fleet has handled **KES 10M+** in payments against a **99% uptime** commitment.
