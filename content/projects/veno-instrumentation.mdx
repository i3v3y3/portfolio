## The problem

Clients kept asking for the same thing wearing different clothes: how full is
the tank, how much power is this drawing. Every one was being quoted weeks of
custom development, and commissioning a bespoke application to read two sensors
is overkill.

## What I built

Sensing hardware for Veno's level sensor and the VeNode energy meter, on a
five-engineer team where I owned embedded hardware design. The metering scales
from a single appliance to a full industrial complex, and readings stream to a
real-time dashboard.

## Choosing the radio per site

Four radio technologies, picked against the site rather than by preference:

| Radio | When it wins |
| --- | --- |
| LoRa | Distance, no mains power, low data rate. A tank in a field. |
| WiFi | Existing infrastructure on site, higher data rate. |
| BLE | Commissioning and local diagnostics from a phone. |
| NFC | Tap-to-configure with no pairing step. |

The interesting constraint is that range and power pull against each other. A
level sensor on a borehole has no mains supply and needs to last on a battery,
which rules out anything that keeps a radio warm.

## Result

Seven products shipped in ten months across school, utility, retail and
municipal deployments, and roughly six weeks of per-deployment engineering
removed from each client install.
