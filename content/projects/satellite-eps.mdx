## The problem

A CubeSat gets one attempt. There is no maintenance window, no second board,
and no way to power-cycle anything by hand. The electrical power system has to
fail in ways that do not take the spacecraft with them, on a budget measured in
single-digit watts.

## What I built

The power distribution board, taken from schematic capture through layout to
fabrication release across a three-month placement. Four regulated rails on
redundant distribution paths, carrying DC-DC conversion, filtering and
overcurrent protection.

## Why six independently protected branches

The failure mode that matters is not a rail browning out, it is one subsystem
faulting and dragging the bus down with it. Splitting distribution into six
branches, each behind its own overcurrent device, means a short in one payload
opens that branch and leaves the other five running. The cost is board area and
part count for protection you hope never operates.

## Why 6 layers

Dedicated power and ground planes. With four switching converters on one board,
a shared plane means each converter's switching noise couples into the others'
feedback paths, and the efficiency you measured on the bench is not what you get
in orbit. Separate planes cost two extra layers and buy predictable behaviour.

## Result

**92% conversion efficiency** against a **5 W** orbit-average budget under bench
characterisation, carried through two design reviews.
