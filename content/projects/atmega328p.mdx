## Why bother

The Atmega328P needs very little around it to run: a supply, a clock, a reset
line, and some way to get code onto it. Carrying a full Arduino Uno around to
use one is mostly carrying connectors and headers you are not going to touch.

## What I built

A **40 x 50 mm** board in Altium, replicating the Uno platform to about **50%**.
Supply, reset button and a blink LED for bring-up, with the schematic, layout
and PDF published so anyone can fork the design.

## The USB bridge decision

The reference design handles USB with a second microcontroller running bridge
firmware. That is flexible — you can reflash it to present as something other
than a serial port — but for a development board it means two parts to source,
two things to program, and a firmware image to keep track of for a job that has
a fixed answer.

I used a dedicated USB-to-UART bridge IC instead. One part, no firmware, and the
serial port appears on the host without anything to configure. The flexibility
lost is flexibility a development board does not use.
